# About

Hi, I'm **Mystical Mahnke**.

I'm an independent developer, founder, and systems builder behind **Helen Kella NeuNuc LLC**, where I'm building **NeuNuc**: adaptive cognitive infrastructure for people, knowledge, AI, workflows, and human variability.

I build from the places existing systems fail.

That means the places where tools lose context, workflows break down, paperwork becomes punishment, people are treated like edge cases, and software expects you to become more organized before it helps you organize.

My work starts from a simple thesis:

> **What is often dismissed as niche is often the expressed nucleus.**

NeuNuc is my attempt to build from that nucleus.

It is not just another productivity app, AI wrapper, or generic automation project. It is an evolving system of tools, agents, workflows, documentation structures, and local-first infrastructure designed around real complexity: neurodivergence, fragmented work, institutional mismatch, recovery systems, civic barriers, creator safety, public records, legal friction, and the need to preserve context across messy human life.

I am building practical infrastructure for things people are usually left to manage alone:

- organizing scattered knowledge, files, projects, and obligations
- turning messy thoughts into usable workflows
- building AI agents that preserve context instead of flattening it
- creating local-first tools, registries, and operating boards
- designing documentation systems that survive real life
- making public systems, paperwork, rights, and records easier to navigate
- building safety-conscious infrastructure for sensitive workflows
- turning lived-system failure into reusable software patterns

Some of this work becomes developer tooling.

Some becomes public tools.

Some becomes research, writing, templates, documentation, and applied systems.

Some becomes the deeper NeuNuc platform: a long-term effort to build software that adapts to people instead of forcing people to adapt to rigid software.

Sponsorship helps me keep building this in public.

It gives me room to turn prototypes into usable tools, document what I learn, publish reusable templates, maintain open experiments, and keep developing ambitious human-centered software without reducing it to whatever is easiest to monetize first.

I am not promising polished perfection on a fixed schedule.

I am promising steady work, honest iteration, public learning, and systems built for the kinds of people and problems most software treats as inconvenient.

If something I build helps you feel more capable, less alone, better prepared, more organized, or more able to make something real, that is the point.

Thank you for supporting independent, weird, serious, human-centered software.
